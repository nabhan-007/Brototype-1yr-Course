GRAMMAR LESSON FOR THIS WEEK
13. IDIOMS
Idioms are phrases that have a meaning different from the literal meanings of the words used. They are expressions that are unique to a particular language and culture, and often they cannot be understood just by looking at the individual words. 
Idioms are used to convey a specific message or feeling, and they add colour and depth to language. For example, the idiom "raining cats and dogs" doesn't mean that animals are falling from the sky; it actually means that it's raining heavily. Similarly, "barking up the wrong tree" doesn't involve any actual dogs; it means to pursue a mistaken or misguided course of action. 
Idioms are common in everyday language, and understanding them can help you become more fluent and natural-sounding in a language Show less
